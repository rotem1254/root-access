You ran a command as root — that is exactly what sudo is for.

Some files belong to the "root" superuser and nobody else may read them, no
matter what permissions you hold. `sudo` lets a trusted user run ONE command as
root after proving who they are with THEIR OWN password. `sudo -l` shows what
you are allowed to run; membership in the "sudo" group here means "anything".

Handle this power carefully on real systems: as root there is nothing to stop a
mistake.
